What if every visitor, parent, and student at University of Layyah could get accurate campus answers in seconds, without digging through menus or waiting for an office to open?

That question became our project for the IEEE South Punjab Hackathon 2026 (Generative AI Coding Challenge): UL AI Assistant, a campus companion built for real people at University of Layyah, not a generic chatbot demo.

This post is our story. The demo, the stack, the bugs that almost broke us, and the lessons that made the product stronger.

What we built
UL AI Assistant helps visitors, parents, and students with:
Admissions guidance and how to apply
Full faculties, departments, and 56 programs aligned with ul.edu.pk
Campus locations and maps (City Campus and Main Campus)
Fees guidance, scholarships, labs, libraries, and bus routes
Live announcements synced from the official website
Chat suggestions while typing, plus pin, rename, and delete chat history

Official source of truth: https://ul.edu.pk

Tech stack
Frontend: React, TypeScript, Vite, Tailwind CSS, Wouter, TanStack Query
Backend: Node.js, Express, TypeScript
Data: Drizzle ORM with PGlite (local Postgres-compatible DB, no Docker required)
AI layer: started with Gemini Flash, explored GORG API, then settled on Ollama (local LLM) with offline knowledge fallback
Knowledge system: structured university KB, RAG-style section retrieval, intent-based professional answers
Website intelligence: scrapers and auto-sync from ul.edu.pk (homepage Academics hierarchy, departments, staff, stories)
Tooling: pnpm monorepo, SSE streaming chat, Windows-friendly local setup

Development journey (and what went wrong)

Phase 1: Idea to working chat
We started with a campus assistant vision. Early builds looked fine, but answers were incomplete or wrong. Spelling mistakes like Layyeh and even University of Lahore slipped in. Computing departments were sometimes shown on the wrong campus. That taught us a hard rule: for university products, accuracy beats clever wording.

How we tackled it: spelling locks, campus location rules, and factual paths for questions like programs, staff, buses, labs, and fees.

Phase 2: The AI provider rollercoaster
This was one of the hardest parts of the project.

We first integrated Gemini Flash API. It worked in early tests, then reality hit:
Free quota exhaustion during repeated demos and testing
Tokens-per-minute (TPM) limits that throttled or blocked replies mid-conversation
Auto-sync and chat load amplifying token usage because too much knowledge was sent with every request

So we switched to GORG API, hoping for a more stable path. That introduced a new wall:
Serious data compression and payload-size pressure
Our university knowledge base was far beyond what we could reliably push through in one go
Large context meant failed or degraded responses, even when the model itself was fine

That forced us to rethink the architecture. We tried multiple alternatives, compared tradeoffs, and stopped treating one big API call with the full KB as a solution.

What finally worked:
Local AI with Ollama for free, unlimited conversational use
Offline knowledge answers when the model is unavailable
Retrieval so only relevant sections enter the prompt
Intent-based replies for factual campus questions

Lesson: generative AI product design is also quota design, context design, and failure-mode design.

Phase 3: Real data from the official website
Manual admin entry was fragile. The site structure (faculties to departments to programs to staff) had to match ul.edu.pk exactly.

Challenges:
Scrapers failed on messy HTML and undefined name parsing
Department pages did not always expose program links cleanly
Fee pages are interactive filters, so inventing fee numbers would have been dishonest
Staff cards needed careful extraction (names, emails, designations)
Auto-sync had to refresh news and stats without wiping carefully structured academic data

How we tackled it: homepage Academics menu parsing for hierarchy, department page staff extraction, rebuild scripts into structured JSON, and clear fee guidance that points users to the official Fee Structure page.

Phase 4: UX that feels professional
A big bug was knowledge dump mode. Asking for all programs returned a wall of raw fields, staff emails, sync timestamps, and visitor tips. It looked unfinished.

How we tackled it: intent-based answer composition. Ask for programs, get programs. Ask for CS location, get location. Clean Markdown, short and useful.

Phase 5: Product polish under pressure
Windows setup issues (ports, PGlite corruption after forced process kills), Vite serving old cached UI, sidebar truncation, and missing pin/rename/delete controls all showed up late.

How we tackled it: clearer run scripts, safe DB reset guidance, forced frontend reloads, and a redesigned chat history with full titles and always-visible Pin, Rename, and Delete.

Key learning points
1. Generative AI is only as good as the grounding data. Ground it in official sources.
2. Free tiers, TPM limits, and payload size are core product constraints, not side notes.
3. When one API fails, do not only swap providers. Redesign how knowledge is retrieved and compressed.
4. Retrieval and intent routing matter more than dumping everything into the prompt.
5. UX bugs destroy trust faster than model quality issues.
6. Scraping real university sites means incomplete HTML, source typos, and pages that cannot be scraped cleanly.
7. Local-first architecture (PGlite + Ollama) makes demos reproducible for students without cloud spend.
8. Team clarity beats solo speed when the problem spans data, backend, frontend, and AI behavior.
